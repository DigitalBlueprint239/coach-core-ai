It gives you time and a clear view of the defense before and after the snap, which is why most spread offenses use it as their base. Shotgun formation diagram Q 5 to 7 yds How the Shotgun Lines Up The QB stands directly behind the center. Most sets put one back beside the QB, roughly even or slightly deeper. Some put a back on each side, and empty sets have none. Why Teams Use It Vision. You never turn away from the defense to drop, so you can watch the safeties and see the rotation at the snap. Time. You start 5 to 7 yards off the ball, so quick throws come out fast and the rush has farther to go. Spread run game. Zone read and RPOs work naturally from the gun, with the QB reading a defender while the back runs alongside. Common Shotgun Sets Double (2x2), two receivers to each side with the back beside the QB. Trips (3x1), three receivers to one side. Bunch, three receivers clustered tight to one side. Empty, five receivers out and nobody in the backfield. Common Questions How far back is the quarterback in the shotgun? Usually 5 to 7 yards behind the center. Why do teams run the shotgun? It lets the QB see the defense without turning away, buys time against the rush and fits spread passing and read-option run games.
