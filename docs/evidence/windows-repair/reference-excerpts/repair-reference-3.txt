Start 7-day free trial No card required The 3-4 defense is a base front in American football that lines up three down linemen, four linebackers and four defensive backs. High school, college and NFL defenses use the 3-4 because four standing linebackers can either rush or drop into coverage, so the offense has to guess which fourth or fifth player is coming on every snap. That flexibility comes with real personnel demands. The nose tackle takes on double-teams on almost every run, the ends have to hold their ground against offensive tackles, and the outside linebackers must set a hard edge, rush the passer and drop into the flat. With two safeties behind it, the 3-4 can play Cover 2, Cover 3 or Cover 4 from the same look. Before you install it, check that your roster has those bodies. Objective Build a base front that controls the run with three linemen, keeps four linebackers free to flow, and disguises which players rush the quarterback. Setup Area Line of scrimmage to about 8 yards deep, tackle box plus the edges Players 7 front players (3 linemen, 4 linebackers) against a scout offensive line and backfield Equipment 1 ball, Stand-up bags or cones for offensive linemen, Colored pinnies for gap assignments Duration 15–20 minutes of front install, then team run fits How it works 1 Align the three down linemen The nose tackle lines up head-up on the center (a 0-technique) or shaded slightly to one side. The two ends align head-up or on the inside shoulder of the offensive tackles (a 4 or 4i-technique), or outside them in a 5-technique, depending on the call. All three keep their eyes on the blocker in front of them. 2 Set the outside linebackers The two outside linebackers align on the edge, outside the tackle or tight end, usually in a two-point stance about a yard off the ball. They own the outside gap on their side, force wide runs back inside and are the most common pass rushers. Against a split receiver with no tight end, many staffs walk them out a couple of yards wider and deeper so they can still reach the flat. 3 Stack the inside linebackers The two inside linebackers align 4–5 yards deep, roughly over the guards. On runs they fill the gaps the linemen leave open; on passes they drop to hook zones or cover backs and tight ends man-to-man. 4 Teach gap control In a two-gap 3-4, each lineman stacks the blocker in front of him with extended arms, reads the ball and makes the tackle in either gap, keeping blockers off the linebackers. In a one-gap 3-4, each lineman attacks a single gap and the linebackers fill the rest. Pick one system per call and name every gap on the board. 5 Choose the rushers On a four-man pressure the three linemen rush with one linebacker, either an outside linebacker off the edge or an inside linebacker through a gap. On a five-man pressure, two linebackers come, such as a crossfire where both inside linebackers cross through the A and B gaps. Because all four linebackers look the same pre-snap, protection has to account for each one. 3-4 front alignment by position (two-gap base; one-gap fronts assign a single gap instead) Position Alignment / technique Gap Main job Nose tackle 0-technique, head-up on the center Both A gaps Hold the double-team so the inside linebackers stay clean Defensive ends 4 or 4i over the tackle, or a 5 outside him B and C gap to his side Stack the tackle, shed to the ball, rush the passer Outside linebackers Two-point stance outside the tackle or tight end C or D gap (contain) Set the edge, rush the passer or drop to the flat Inside linebackers Over the guards, 4–5 yards deep Fill the open A or B gap Fit the run, drop to hook zones or cover backs Safeties Two high, 10–12 yards deep Alley support Play the called coverage and fill late in the run game Coaching points ● The nose tackle wins by not getting moved: low pad level, hands inside, and hold the point against the center and guard double-team. ● Outside linebackers never let the ball carrier get outside them; spill or squeeze, but set the edge. ● Inside linebackers read guard to back: a pulling or down-blocking guard tells them where the ball is going before the running back declares. ● Every rush and drop starts from the same stance and depth so the offense cannot tip off who is coming. ● Against spread sets with a light box, rotate a safety down or bump a linebacker over the slot rather than leaving five defenders against five blockers and a back. Variations 3-4 Under Shift the front away from the tight end, move the weak-side end into a 3-technique and walk the strong outside linebacker up on the tight end. It gives single-gap linemen easier one-on-one blocks and plays more like a four-man front. Slanting 3-4 for youth teams Line the three linemen head-up on the center and tackles, then slant all three one gap on the call, away from the side the strong outside linebacker aligns. The linebackers fill the gaps the slant leaves behind. Each player learns one rule, which suits teams that cannot yet two-gap. Start 7-day free trial No card required Frequently asked questions What is the difference between a 3-4 and a 4-3 defense? + A 3-4 defense uses three down linemen and four linebackers, while a 4-3 uses four linemen and three linebackers. The 3-4 disguises pass rushers better, since any of four standing linebackers can rush. The 4-3 controls more gaps at the line and asks less of one nose tackle. The 3-4 needs a big nose and athletic outside linebackers; the 4-3 needs four good linemen. Is the 3-4 defense good for youth American football? + The 3-4 defense can work in youth American football, but it asks a lot of young players. The nose tackle faces frequent double-teams and linebackers must read quickly. Youth coaches usually succeed with a slanting or one-gap version, simple assignments and a strong nose. Teams short on linebackers often prefer a 4-3, 4-4 or 5-3 front. What is the weakness of the 3-4 defense? + The main weakness of the 3-4 defense is a light box against spread formations: with only three linemen on the ball, offenses can match five blockers on five box defenders and run. Trips sets and quick slot receivers also stress the outside linebackers in coverage. Defenses answer with a safety in the box, nickel personnel or slants that confuse blocking assignments.
